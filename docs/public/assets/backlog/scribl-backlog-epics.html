scribl backlog, epics and features

Project: scribl. Status: draft for team review. Updated: 2026-08-20.

Epics and features for the eight-week Scribl build that starts 2026-08-24. Shape is seven working days, not two weeks, then three two-week build sprints. See "The calendar" below for the confirmed dates. No user stories on this page by direction; decomposition happens at sprint planning with the people who will do the work.

Prioritization is the kickoff board of 2026-08-19, read verbatim. Its three bands are GREEN ("FIRST, carries the daily loop"), ORANGE ("NEXT, the real conversation, what earns a sprint slot"), and GRAY ("OUTSIDE these 8 weeks, each has a later home, none are dropped"). Every gray epic below carries a named later home, because the board's own words are that none are dropped.

Two epics on this page are not on the board. E06 (QA foundation and standards) and E07 (build, signing and tester distribution) are additions, marked as such in their metadata, and the reason each is here is argued in its description. One epic, E15, is not work at all; it is the capability the new designs took off the product surface, already built and switched off, recorded so nobody reads the reduction as a deletion.

Risks and things I would change

The green band does not fit, and neither lane has the slack to absorb it. 118 lane-days of green work against 99 available, 154 against 99 once the orange band is included. iOS is 11 days over on green and backend is 14 days over, so this is not a problem one extra engineer solves. The arithmetic and the three options are in the capacity section. My recommendation is to plan the green band only, let the four client-blocked features slip out of the three sprints, and request the two extra engineers in parallel rather than instead.

Q5 blocks more of this page than any other question. No Apple Developer account means no signing identity, no TestFlight, and no demo build. It blocks four features across E07 and it is not something Bounteous can close on its own.

The board and the client disagree about Claude and the daily prompt. The board sticky says "Daily prompt (Claude)"; the client decided prompts stay curated with Claude limited to drafting for editorial review. E03 follows the client. Somebody should correct the sticky, because a backlog that reads as authorization to generate prompts at runtime contradicts the one thing Eric Rice was emphatic about.

The board and the designs disagree about voice. The frames put Record on two screens; the board cut voice memos from the MLP. E04-F3 ships the write path only and E15-F3 holds the specified pipeline. This is a client decision (Q18) and it should not be resolved by whoever builds the screen.

Onboarding is a live design sticky and not in the FIRST group. E02-F4 is 5 of the 41 green-band iOS-days. If onboarding really is not first-group work, that is 5 days of relief on the critical path, and if it is first-group work then the board should say so. Q11 tracks it; it is worth ten minutes at sprint 1 planning.

Two epics on this page are additions. E06 and E07 are not on the board. E06 was endorsed directly by Rob Forshier. E07 is my own addition and the argument for it is in its description: three Wednesday demos require a build reaching a human being, and nothing on the board covers that.

Nothing here re-opens the eleven ADRs. Where an ADR decided something, this page cites it and moves on. Where the ADR review itself left a gate open (the Aurora versus DynamoDB confirmation, moderation fail policy, Claude hosting), the affected feature says so rather than picking silently. Three things the architecture docs genuinely do not cover, and which this backlog therefore does not assume: no CI tool is named anywhere (E01-F7 recommends one and flags it), no migration path exists from this phase's Lambda and Fargate design to the future-state EKS design, and no observability plan exists for this phase, which is why E01-F8 is deliberately the CloudWatch-only floor.
