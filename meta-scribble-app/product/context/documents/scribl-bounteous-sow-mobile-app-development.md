1.3. Overview of the Initiative 
 
Problem and Solution:  
Scribl empowers people. A single daily prompt invites each person to make one small thing by hand and 
share it with the people who matter to them. Scribl's guiding word is connection, and its guiding principle 
is to keep it simple. 
 
Before committing to a full build, Scribl needs two things: a reliable read on how quickly a product of this 
kind can be built, and something real in hand to evaluate. This first phase delivers both. Vendor will apply 
its AI-native delivery method to Scribl's prioritized backlog and produce a running prototype of the product 
on  the  architecture  a  full  build  would  use,  so  that both  the  learning  and the  code  carry  forward into  a 
follow-on phase. 
 
Objectives:  
Client and Vendor aim to establish the velocity of an agentic development team on a real product. Velocity, 
meaning  how  much  usable  functionality  the  team delivers  within the  fixed budget  and  timeline, is  the 
primary success metric for this phase. 
Within the term, Vendor will conduct a two (2) week Shape phase followed by three (3) two-week build 
sprints against Scribl's prioritized backlog, delivering a running prototype at the end of each build sprint 
and culminating in a final running prototype Scribl can use and demonstrate. Vendor will also deliver a 
velocity summary and recommended options for a follow-on phase, which the parties will pursue subject 
to additional funding. 
This engagement is time-boxed to what the team can deliver within the fixed fee of $100,000 set out in 
Section 2.1. Scope follows the budget and the timeline rather than a fixed feature list.  
 
1.4. Approach 
 
Vendor  will  run  a  velocity-based  engagement  using  its  AI-native  delivery  method,  Bounteous  ARC,  in 
which design and engineering move together from idea to running code. AI generates the volume of code, 
tests, and pipelines while Vendor's engineers own the architecture, product intent, and quality bar. This 
describes  Vendor's  delivery  method;  the  in-product  AI  features  of  the  Scribl  application  are  product 
functionality, delivered from the backlog like any other feature.  
 
Shape (weeks 1 to 2). The engagement opens with a two (2) week Shape phase that resolves the highest-
risk unknowns before build begins: targeted technical spikes, a locked and estimated backlog, the finalized 
target architecture, and a clickable prototype of the core experience. Shape produces the baseline against 
which velocity is measured.

Build  (weeks  3  to  8).  The  team  then  works  in  three  (3)  two-week  sprints  against  Scribl's  prioritized 
backlog, and each sprint ends with the running prototype Scribl can use, built on the architecture a full 
build would use. Rather than committing to a fixed feature list, the team optimizes for how much usable, 
high-value functionality it can deliver within the fixed budget and timeline. Scribl sets and re-prioritizes 
the backlog between sprints, so the most valuable work is always built first, and the running prototype 
reflects  Scribl's  current priorities. A "running prototype"  means  a  functional build  of the  application 
demonstrating the backlog items completed during the applicable sprint in a test environment reasonably 
accessible to Client. 
 
Continuous  validation  and  CI/CD  run  from  day  one,  so  quality  holds  as  pace  increases.  At  each  sprint 
review Scribl sees running prototype, gives feedback, and re-prioritizes; at the end of week eight the team 
hands  over  the  final  working  prototype  along  with  a  velocity  summary  and  recommended  options  for 
additional follow-on phase.
