Karuna completed analysis on SCRIBL-28 and posted questions on it, is

deriving test cases for both stories.

Rob reminded the team to connect Atlassian in their Claude desktop app, since
he had just merged in changes. He disclosed that his own gating had
accidentally blocked the whole team from making backlog changes in Jira,
which Karuna caught and told him about that morning; Karuna has raised a
permissions request and hopes it resolves by Monday. Rob asked her to also
tell the rest of the team, and said he will add the note to the access list
Pramod created.

Shubhankar posted a device-procurement proposal (start with a single, basic
device rather than a full fleet). His own tooling is fully set up: both
Android and iOS emulators run, and he sees no blocker to running code or
doing MVP development. He picked up SCRIBL-8 and opened a PR in the meta
Scribl app repo with an architectural proposal, and asked Rob to review it.
His stated strategy going forward: build each module close to parity on both
platforms even though Android is not yet prioritized, so a later Android
push needs less turnaround time, and test each push against both platforms.
He will keep working SCRIBL-8, move to SCRIBL-18 once the device-procurement
question is settled, and plans to pick up SCRIBL-76 next.

On SCRIBL-76, Shubhankar reported the Apple developer account is set up and
one application already exists, currently named "Scribl Prod". He raised
whether the App Store should show that name or just "Scribl" to end users.
Eric confirmed the intent is "Scribl" only; the current name came from the
doc he was working from. Shubhankar laid out the tradeoff: a single
application kept carefully separated between test and production pushes, or
two separate applications, one purely for dev/test and one for production,
which is cleaner but means working two pipelines. Rob proposed a naming
convention -- something like "scribl-dev" for the QA/prototype build and a
plain "scribl" for production, so the production app never carries the word
"production" in it. Matthew asked whether the beta group testing the app
would be on a separate build that will not change under them; Rob confirmed
that is exactly the split: a testing/playground artifact carrying the latest
features, and a hardened production artifact that only gets the best of
what passes QA, with beta users eventually getting both. Rob asked
Shubhankar to write up the proposal, with benefits and turnaround-time
tradeoffs, and bring it to the full team next week rather than deciding on
this call, especially once the architect, Pankaj, is back.

Rob noted he had also moved the meta repo into the Scribl GitHub org the day
before, so Scribl now hosts both the official meta repo and the Scribl
mobile app repo the team has been building in. He said he would demo where
the app stands in the later meeting with Eric and Matt.

Eric's one open item: he gave Pramod an IAM user account into Scribl's AWS
development organization so Pramod can get in and look around, but the two
sides still need to connect Bounteous's team to that specific account.
Pramod has full IAM access and will pick it up Monday; Eric will be
available Sunday evening and most of Monday to work the connection with him
on his time.

In after-items, Karuna asked, on behalf of a question raised the day before,
whether Eric's side has BrowserStack credentials for running automated tests
across browser and device combinations. Eric was unsure and had no answer.
Rob explained the tool for the group (a licensed cross-browser/device test
runner covering both simulators and real devices) and suggested Karuna write
up a proposal for why the team should use it, to run past Scribl in case they
need to buy the licenses. Eric agreed to help with that write-up.
